iD RG

I am a paleoanthropologist and zooarchaeologist interested in human evolution, particularly the dietary behavior of early Homo. My doctoral research at Colorado State University focuses on hominin material culture and feeding ecology at Olduvai Gorge, Tanzania.

I grew up in the rural highlands of Ethiopia and studied at Addis Ababa University before working at the National Museum of Ethiopia. I have been fortunate to work on fossil collections and field projects in Ethiopia, Tanzania, and the United States.

Research

My work uses bone surface modifications and actualistic experiments to understand how early hominins made and used tools, obtained food, and interacted with carnivores.

Actualistic Experiments

Using actualistic experiments, such as retouching stone tools with bone, to develop a quantitative method for identifying bone retoucher marks, and applying it to Early Stone Age archaeological sites in Africa.

Related work:2025 talk2024 poster (ResearchGate)

Hominin Feeding Ecology at Olduvai Gorge

Examining hominin feeding ecology across the Oldowan to Acheulean transition at Olduvai Gorge, with a focus on the scale of hominin carnivory at Bed II sites.

Related work:Frontiers 2026JHE 2026

Middle Stone Age Blue Highways Project

Member of the team working at Shinfa-Metema 1, northwest Ethiopia, on hominin foraging around the time of the Toba supereruption.

Related work:Nature 2024
